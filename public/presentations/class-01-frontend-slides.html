SLIDE 04 / 08 STAGE 2 (4:15–4:40 PM) • HARDWARE ANATOMY SAFETY FIRST: WIRE WITH POWER OFF

Inside the 400-Point Breadboard & LED Polarity

If you place both legs of an LED into the same 5-hole vertical column, the hidden metal clip creates a dead short! Here is how the clips actually run:

RULE 01 • POWER RAILS (+ AND -)

Horizontal Power Highways

The long top and bottom strips marked with Red (+) and Blue (-) lines are connected HORIZONTALLY all the way across the board to distribute +5V and GND.

RULE 02 • TERMINAL COLUMNS (A–E & F–J)

5-Hole Vertical Groups & The Center Trench

Each numbered column (1 to 30) is connected VERTICALLY in groups of 5 holes (a-b-c-d-e and f-g-h-i-j). The center ravine completely isolates the top half from the bottom half.

RULE 03 • LED ONE-WAY VALVE

Anode (+) Long Leg vs. Cathode (-) Short Leg

An LED is a diode—current only flows one way! Connect the Long Leg (Anode +) toward +5V (via 220Ω resistor) and the Short Leg / Flat Rim (Cathode -) to Ground (GND).

Always place the two legs of an LED or resistor into TWO DIFFERENT numbered columns!

4-Step Power-Off Wiring Checklist

Wire 1: Arduino 5V (or Pin D8) ➔ Breadboard Red Rail (+)
Wire 2: Arduino GND ➔ Breadboard Blue Rail (-)
Wire 3: 220Ω Resistor (Red-Red-Brown) from Red Rail (+) to Row 10a
Wire 4: LED Long Leg (+) in Row 10c, Short Leg (-) in Blue Rail (-)

WHY WE NEED THE 220Ω RESISTOR

Protecting the Semiconductor Junction

A Red LED drops $V_f = 2.0\text{V}$ and wants $\sim 15\text{mA}$. With a $5.0\text{V}$ supply:
R = (5.0V - 2.0V) / 0.0136A = 220 Ω

SLIDE 06 / 08 ARDUINO C++ FIRMWARE WALKTHROUGH PIN D8 OUTPUT CONTROL

Commanding Electricity with C++ Code

Once your steady 5V LED circuit passes inspection, move the positive jumper wire from 5V to Digital Pin 8 so your code can switch it ON and OFF!

01 • VOID SETUP() — RUNS ONCE

pinMode(8, OUTPUT);

Configures Digital Pin 8 inside the ATmega328P chip as a power-supplying output pin ready to drive 5V or 0V.

02 • VOID LOOP() — RUNS FOREVER

digitalWrite(8, HIGH); & LOW

HIGH switches Pin 8 to +5.0 Volts (turning the LED ON). LOW switches Pin 8 to 0 Volts (turning the LED OFF).

03 • MILLISECOND TIMING

delay(500); (1000 ms = 1 Second)

Pauses the microcontroller for 500 milliseconds (half a second) so the human eye can clearly see each flash.

ROBOBEE_CLASS_01_EXTERNAL_LED_BLINK.INO 
// RoboBee Innovators — Class 01: External LED Beacon
const int LED_PIN = 8; // Digital Pin 8 connected via 220Ω resistor

void setup() {
  // 1. Configure Pin 8 as an electrical OUTPUT once at boot
  pinMode(LED_PIN, OUTPUT);
}

void loop() {
  // 2. Turn LED ON (+5.0V) and wait 500 milliseconds
  digitalWrite(LED_PIN, HIGH);
  delay(500);

  // 3. Turn LED OFF (0.0V) and wait 500 milliseconds
  digitalWrite(LED_PIN, LOW);
  delay(500);
}

EXIT CHECK QUESTION 01

How are the holes connected inside a breadboard?

Compare the long top/bottom power rails (+ / -) to the numbered terminal columns (a–e and f–j).

👆 Click Card to Reveal Answer
Answer: Power rails (+ and -) are connected HORIZONTALLY along the edges, while terminal rows (a–e and f–j) are connected VERTICALLY in 5-hole groups separated by the center trench.
EXIT CHECK QUESTION 02

How do you identify the Anode (+) and Cathode (-) of an LED?

And why does the LED stay completely dark if you plug it in backward?

👆 Click Card to Reveal Answer
Answer: The Anode (+) is the LONGER leg and connects toward +5V; the Cathode (-) is the SHORTER leg (with the flat plastic rim) and connects to GND. An LED is a one-way semiconductor diode!
EXIT CHECK QUESTION 03

What happens if you connect an LED to 5V without a 220Ω resistor?

Use Ohm's Law ($I = V / R$) to explain why the resistor is mandatory.

👆 Click Card to Reveal Answer
Answer: Without a current-limiting resistor, resistance $R \approx 0$, causing a massive current spike ($>100\text{mA}$) that instantly overheats and burns out the microscopic LED junction!
